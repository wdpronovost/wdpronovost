# Comparable AI-career personal websites

Inspected live on 2026-07-28. Candidates were evaluated for overlap with Billy Pronovost's combination of technology leadership, hands-on software/product building, design judgment, AI-assisted practice, systems thinking, and public artifacts. Visual references were checked on desktop and mobile where available.

## Closest overall references

### 1. Eugene Yan — https://eugeneyan.com/
**Positioning:** Applied scientist at Anthropic working to bridge the field and frontier; writes, speaks, prototypes, and teaches.

**Why it matters:** The clearest proof architecture in the set. One direct positioning paragraph branches immediately into writing, talks, prototypes, teaching, and a current feed. Visitors can verify several dimensions of credibility without reading a long biography.

**Borrow:** Positioning → proof lanes → recent evidence. Make prototypes a first-class navigation item.

**Do not copy:** The homepage is visually generic and text-heavy. It solves career communication, not Billy's need for a distinctive authored experience.

### 2. Geoffrey Litt — https://www.geoffreylitt.com/
**Positioning:** Designs and prototypes malleable software and AI-assisted programming environments; currently at Notion, previously Ink & Switch and MIT HCI.

**Why it matters:** Closest intellectual overlap. He turns a coherent point of view about software, thinking, and human agency into prototype thumbnails and essays.

**Borrow:** Name Billy's territory clearly, group projects by the questions they investigate, and pair every thesis with a working artifact.

**Do not copy:** The pale-yellow research-CV presentation and exposed email treatment. It is not optimized for a hiring-manager conversion path.

### 3. Maggie Appleton — https://maggieappleton.com/
**Positioning:** Designer, anthropologist, and developer exploring AI and software engineering; makes visual essays about programming, design, and anthropology.

**Why it matters:** Best example of building a memorable interdisciplinary identity instead of competing on the generic title “AI expert.” The garden taxonomy makes her way of thinking visible.

**Borrow:** A concise interdisciplinary thesis; a living knowledge system of essays, notes, patterns, projects, and current work.

**Do not copy:** Her oversized literary serif, warm garden metaphor, and illustrated anthropology identity are specific to her and would be visibly derivative.

### 4. Amelia Wattenberger — https://wattenberger.com/
**Positioning:** Creates novel web interfaces, turns data into meaning, and uses AI to empower developers; Principal Research Engineer at GitHub.

**Why it matters:** Strongest visual evidence that an AI-adjacent technical career site can feel personal, experimental, and unmistakably authored.

**Borrow:** Custom graphic language, current-research framing, interactive writing, and the confidence to make the homepage itself an interface experiment.

**Do not copy:** The decorative hero delays professional signal on mobile. Billy's role and value need to appear before atmosphere.

### 5. Peter Steinberger — https://steipete.me/
**Positioning:** Builds AI-powered tools from Swift roots to web frontiers; publishes every commit for others to fork and remix.

**Why it matters:** Strong analog for an experienced builder whose prior career naturally evolves into AI tooling. The site makes the transition feel continuous rather than like a reinvention.

**Borrow:** “Existing roots → current AI frontier” narrative, public build record, practical writing, and open repositories.

**Do not copy:** A chronological blog alone will not communicate Billy's leadership, product portfolio, or methods quickly enough.

### 6. Simon Willison — https://simonwillison.net/ and https://tools.simonwillison.net/
**Positioning:** Independent open-source developer documenting LLMs, data, software, and security while continually shipping small tools.

**Why it matters:** Strongest public-lab-notebook model. Authority comes from frequency, specificity, tools, transparent transcripts, and ongoing technical judgment—not from self-description.

**Borrow:** A dated lab feed, small usable tools, explicit build notes, and visible continuity between experiments.

**Do not copy:** The dense weblog interface assumes an established audience and is not a strong cold-visitor hiring path.

### 7. Linus Lee — https://thesephist.com/
**Positioning:** Researches AI interpretability, interfaces, deployment, knowledge representation, and creative work; builds tools for clearer thinking.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates how one long-term research statement can unite writing, prototypes, talks, projects, and employment history.

**Borrow:** Plainspoken research agenda and lightweight navigation across posts, projects, and a live stream.

**Do not copy:** The text-only research aesthetic undersells Billy's visual/product judgment and hands-on demonstrations.

### 8. Hamel Husain — https://hamel.dev/
**Positioning:** Applied AI engineering and evals practitioner, consultant, teacher, and open-source contributor.

**Why it matters:** Best example of owning a precise operating lane and repeatedly proving it through practical writing, OSS, courses, and real interventions.

**Borrow:** Name a specific lane Billy can credibly own—such as human-AI operating systems, AI enablement, or accountable AI workflows—and accumulate evidence around it.

**Do not copy:** “Evals” is appropriately narrow for Hamel but too ML-specialized for Billy's broader leadership/product story.

### 9. Jason Liu — https://jxnl.co/
**Positioning:** Developer Experience Engineer on OpenAI Codex; creator of Instructor; writes practical playbooks on RAG, agents, context engineering, evals, and product improvement.

**Why it matters:** Strong credibility stack: frontier role + open-source artifact + practical playbooks + personal operating manual.

**Borrow:** One flagship artifact, one clearly labeled “Start Here” route, and highly practical playbooks.

**Do not copy:** The current OpenAI role and investor identity make the site less comparable as a career-transition artifact.

### 10. Paras Chopra — https://www.invertedpassion.com/
**Positioning:** Experienced founder now building an AI lab, Lossfunk; maintains projects, essays, a knowledge garden, and mental models.

**Why it matters:** Another credible “experienced operator moving into AI” example. The transition is stated in one plain sentence and sits within a longer builder history.

**Borrow:** Treat AI as the current chapter of an established practice, not as a sudden identity replacement.

**Do not copy:** The site is visually generic and uses emoji as navigational assets.

## Useful “do not follow” reference

### Nate Jones — https://www.natebjones.com/
The positioning is clear—AI strategy and product development without hype—but the centered dark hero, neon cyan, pill badge, multiple repeated CTAs, testimonial-card strip, and glowing navigation treatment reproduce many of the generic AI-template signals Billy is explicitly trying to escape.

## Recommended synthesis for wdpronovost.com

No single site should be copied. The strongest synthesis is:

1. **Eugene Yan's information architecture:** one clear position followed by independent proof lanes.
2. **Geoffrey Litt's thesis-to-prototype model:** a worldview made concrete through working software.
3. **Maggie Appleton's authored taxonomy:** notes, patterns, experiments, and essays as a visible thinking system.
4. **Amelia Wattenberger's distinctiveness:** the page itself demonstrates interface judgment.
5. **Simon Willison and Peter Steinberger's public build trace:** dated tools, decisions, commits, and lessons.

For Billy, that suggests a site organized around **Work / Experiments / Field Notes / Operating Systems / About**, with the live work and current AI operating practice visible before biography. The visual system should be developed from Billy's actual artifacts and operating method rather than from another person's palette, typography, or metaphor.
